02 — About the role

What I design

I design the boundary between human life and intelligent systems.

By 2036, AI no longer waits to be asked.

  • It anticipates.
  • It recommends.
  • It monitors.
  • It negotiates.
  • It optimises.
  • It interrupts.

My work is deciding when that intelligence has earned a person's attention, and when it hasn't. Not because AI should become less capable — but because it should become more intentional about when it acts, speaks, recommends, interrupts and disappears.

Intervene

When something genuinely requires human attention.

Defer

When something can wait.

Suppress

When an interaction creates little value.

Disappear

When technology should leave the human experience untouched.

Every AI interaction has a cost — not only in attention, but in computation, electricity, cooling and water. I weigh three things against each other before anything reaches a person:

Human attention+ Human autonomy+ Computational resources

AI should augment human judgement, not replace it.

More capability does not always mean a better experience.

Technology should earn the right to interrupt.

Resource efficiency is part of interaction design.

04 — Design philosophy

The design of nothing

Design has traditionally been rewarded for creating more — more features, more interactions, more engagement, more personalisation. Disengagement design starts from a different question: does this interaction need to exist at all?

Don't notify.
Don't interrupt.
Don't optimise.
Don't personalise.
Don't generate.
Don't record.
Don't intervene.

Sometimes the designed outcome is nothing.

03 — Selected work
01

The presence mode

AI wearable / smart glasses company
Context

AI glasses have become the primary way people navigate the physical world — continuously reading surroundings and offering navigation, recommendations, social information and health prompts.

Problem

The system can detect hundreds of potentially useful things a day. Constant intervention was keeping people from experiencing the physical world directly.

"When should the smartest glasses say nothing?"
Design

A layered intervention system that classifies everything the glasses notice, from immediate intervention down to full suppression, plus a dedicated Presence Mode that deliberately minimises recommendations, advertising, social information and behavioural prompts while keeping emergency access intact.

02

The quiet shift

AI-enabled workplace platform
Context

Workers now run several AI systems at once — productivity, manager, learning, HR, communication and personal assistants, each optimising for its own slice of the day.

Problem

Continuous optimisation, from every direction at once, had created cognitive overload.

"How can AI increase productivity without colonising the entire working day?"
Design

A system of intervention boundaries: no-interruption periods, AI-free meetings, an end-of-work shutdown protocol, notification batching, and clear handoff rules between systems.

Principle

Optimise the work, not every minute of the worker.

03

Compute worthy

AI wearable / personal AI platform
Context

Every recommendation, prediction and interaction requires computation. As AI demand grows, energy, water and compute have become increasingly contested resources.

Problem

Not every interaction is worth the computational resources required to generate it.

"Should this interaction happen at all?"
Design

A compute-to-attention framework that weighs the value of an interaction against the attention it asks for and the cost of generating it, then routes each one: handled locally, deferred, batched, suppressed, or escalated to more powerful intelligence only when the value justifies it.

In 2036, digital interaction is no longer treated as immaterial. Every exchange runs through a physical lifecycle — and it's worth asking, at the end of it, whether the interaction was worth creating.
Sense→ Process→ Generate→ Deliver→ Consume→ Discard

The most sustainable AI interaction is sometimes the one that never happens.

06 — How I work

Method

01
Map

Map every possible interaction between the person and the system.

02
Question

Does the person need this? Does the system need to do this? Does it need to happen now?

03
Classify

Sort each interaction: intervene, defer, batch, suppress, or disappear.

04
Simulate

Use AI to generate and stress-test possible intervention strategies at scale.

05
Test

Test the behaviour in real physical environments, not simulations alone.

06
Measure

Track what changed for the person and the system.

Attention saved Interruptions avoided Autonomy preserved Computation avoided
07
Set boundaries

Turn findings into product rules, interaction protocols and system behaviour that hold after I leave the room.

07 — My client in 2036

A wearable-tech company building AI glasses that mediate the physical world

The glasses continuously interpret their surroundings, talk to other AI systems, and offer contextual assistance. The company's traditional product teams keep asking how to make them more useful. I ask a different question: does useful mean it deserves someone's attention?

"The AI that knows when to leave you alone."

More intelligence → More intentional intelligence
The glasses can always do something. My job is deciding what deserves attention, what can wait, what should stay invisible, and when a person should get their full autonomy back — while balancing usefulness against attention, autonomy and resource consumption.
